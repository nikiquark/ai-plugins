# KTH Innovation Readiness Level: criteria for software products

Paraphrased and adapted from the KTH Innovation Readiness Level™ (KTH Innovation, Stockholm, kth.se/innovation). The original covers deep tech as well. The technology axis here uses its software reading. "Evidence" lists what counts in `docs/idea/`.

## CRL: Customer readiness

| Level | Criteria | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A hunch that a need exists. No clear idea who the customers are or what their problem is; little knowledge of the market | `brief.md` |
| 2 | Secondary research done. The need is described specifically; possible customers and their problems are named. Solution ideas, if any, are speculative | `customers.md`, `market.md` |
| 3 | First direct contact: a few potential customers or industry experts gave feedback. Segments and problem hypotheses are sharper | Evidence notes of first conversations |
| 4 | The problem and its importance are confirmed by several customers (roughly 5–10 in B2B, more in B2C; fewer if the market is concentrated). Segmentation is detailed. A first product hypothesis exists | Evidence notes; B → C gate metrics |
| 5 | Customers show interest in a solution whose core features are confirmed to solve their problem (first problem–solution fit). Relationships with target customers exist; first customers are chosen | Evidence notes; prototype or landing feedback |
| 6 | Customers tested the product and confirmed its value (problem–solution fit validated). Partnerships with pilot customers or key players in the value chain. A first sales process is defined | Pilot results, letters of intent |
| 7 | First sales or paid test sales; customers in extended testing (first signs of product–market fit). Sales effort ramps up | Paid pilots, pre-orders |
| 8 | Products sold to a few customers; enough of them confirmed willingness to pay (product–market fit validated). The real buyers and decision makers are known. A repeatable sales process exists | Revenue, PMF survey, retention |
| 9 | Sales are widespread, repeatable and scalable; the company focuses on growth | Growth metrics |

## BRL: Business model readiness

| Level | Criteria | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A vague business concept. Little insight into market size or competition | `brief.md` |
| 2 | The concept is described in a structured form (need, approach, benefit, competition). Markets are identified at a high level with a rough TAM. Some competitors or alternatives are listed | `preflight.md`, `issue-tree.md` |
| 3 | A business model draft (canvas without revenues and costs). Market sized as TAM and SAM. A complete overview of direct and indirect competitors and alternatives | `market.md`, `competitors.md`, `strategy.md` |
| 4 | A full canvas including revenues and costs. First bottom-up projections show market potential and economic viability. A feasible market share is assessed (SOM, barriers to entry). Differentiation is analyzed | `strategy.md` (model, unit economics), `market.md` (SOM) |
| 5 | Parts of the model are tested with customers and updated. A detailed revenue model with pricing hypotheses. Competitive position and differentiation are confirmed by market feedback | Evidence notes; pricing survey |
| 6 | The full model, including pricing, is verified with customers through test sales or similar. Projections for 1–3 years rest on grounded data | Pre-orders at the planned price, updated projections |
| 7 | Product–market fit: significant interest and usage, customers pay. Revenue versus cost projections are validated by sales data. Scaling is being prepared | Sales data, unit economics from real numbers |
| 8 | Metrics show the model holds and can scale (acquisition costs are under control). Channels are in place. The model is fine-tuned for more revenue | LTV / CAC, payback |
| 9 | The model is final; the business scales with recurring revenue and is profitable and sustainable | Financials |

## TRL: Technology readiness (software reading)

| Level | Criteria | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Basic principles known: the algorithms, data sources or APIs the product would rely on exist and are documented | `strategy.md` feasibility notes |
| 2 | The technical concept is formulated: architecture sketch, critical components named, build-versus-buy for each. Feasibility is argued on paper; key technical risks listed | `strategy.md` feasibility section, `risks.md` |
| 3 | Proof of concept of the critical function: a spike or script shows the riskiest part works on realistic sample data | Repository or demo, evidence note |
| 4 | Core components integrated and working together in a development environment (a working prototype with partial data) | Prototype demo |
| 5 | Components validated in a realistic environment: real data volumes, real integrations, staging deployment | Staging metrics |
| 6 | A representative prototype of the whole system works on full-scale realistic problems; engineering feasibility shown | Pilot deployment |
| 7 | The system runs in the operational environment with real users under real load, security and usage conditions | Production with pilot customers |
| 8 | The final system is complete and qualified: all functionality tested in operational scenarios, documentation complete | Release, test reports |
| 9 | Proven in sustained operation: repeatable, supported, maintained | Uptime, support history |

## TMRL: Team readiness

| Level | Criteria | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Typically one person; little insight into which competencies and resources are needed | `brief.md` |
| 2 | First idea of which competencies, people or partners are needed | `brief.md` |
| 3 | Some needed competencies are present; the needed ones are defined, gaps identified, and a near-term plan (< 1 year) to fill them exists | `brief.md` team section, `strategy.md` resourcing |
| 4 | A champion drives the project. Several competencies are present. Recruiting or partnering for the gaps has started. Roles, ownership and commitment are being discussed | `[user]` evidence |
| 5 | A founding team with the main competencies, spending significant time. Ownership agreed (signed), roles and goals aligned | Agreement, `[user]` evidence |
| 6 | A complementary, committed team with both business and technical competencies for the near term. Advisors considered. Low dependence on any single person | Team description |
| 7 | Culture and roles in place, the team develops proactively; a recruitment plan for about two years | Hiring plan |
| 8 | Clear leadership with relevant experience; a working board and advisors; long-term recruiting under way | Board, hires |
| 9 | A high-performing, well-structured organization that is sustained over time | — |

## IPRL: IPR readiness (optional)

| Level | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 1 | A guess that the work may contain protectable IP (code, data, designs, know-how, trademarks). Ownership and agreements unclear |
| 2 | The forms of possible IP are mapped. Ownership is clarified and under the team's control (who wrote the code, under which agreements and licenses) |
| 3 | Key IP described in enough detail; own search of the field and of existing IP (prior art, competing trademarks) |
| 4 | Protectability confirmed by a professional search; decided what to protect and why; alternatives (trade secrets, trademark) chosen where patents do not fit |
| 5 | A draft IP strategy; basic agreements secure ownership (assignments, contributor agreements); first filing (trademark, software registration or patent) |
| 6 | IP strategy in place and linked to the business; positive response on filings; first freedom-to-operate assessment |
| 7 | All relevant IP filed (more trademarks, designs, patents in other jurisdictions) |
| 8 | IP strategy fully implemented and managed; first grants; fuller freedom-to-operate analysis |
| 9 | Strong protection in force in the relevant markets, maintained |

## FRL: Funding readiness (optional)

| Level | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 1 | A vague idea; no view of how much money is needed, for what, or from where |
| 2 | The concept is described with a first value proposition. Funding needs for the first milestones (3–6 months) and the options for them are mapped |
| 3 | A well-described concept and a verification plan with hypotheses and goals. Basic knowledge of funding types. First small non-dilutive funding or own budget secured for verification |
| 4 | A short pitch and a written presentation of the business. A plan of funding needs and sources for 12–18 months |
| 5 | An investor pitch tested and refined; supporting financial projections. Grants or loans applied for. Openness to equity funding |
| 6 | An improved pitch with business and financials. Decided to seek equity; the first offer (amount, valuation, use of funds) is defined; first investor contacts |
| 7 | The team presents a solid investment case (prototype, traction, market, scalability). A full business plan with milestones. Talks with investors around a concrete offer |
| 8 | The company is in order (ownership, bookkeeping, documents). Term-sheet-level talks with interested investors |
| 9 | Investment closed and received. Future needs considered continuously |
